# Capability Heat Map

**Use when:** you need to connect business strategy to technology investment, justify a rationalization case, or settle an argument about priorities with evidence.
**Time:** 2 hours of preparation plus a 2-hour workshop per division.
**Output:** a two-level capability map with importance, maturity, technology dependence, and performance scores — plotted as a heat map.

---

## Instructions

1. **Build the hierarchy first.** Eight to twelve domains, each with five to fifteen capabilities. A capability is what the business can do ("price a parcel"), not what it bought ("the rating engine").
2. **Score each capability** on the four attributes below. Use the definitions, not your instincts.
3. **Co-create with the business.** IT alone produces a data model; the business produces a shared language.
4. **Plot importance against maturity.** Act on the quadrants.
5. **Assign an owner** to every high-importance, low-maturity capability. Unowned gaps do not close.

---

## Scoring definitions

| Attribute | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strategic importance** | Necessary but not noticed by customers or regulators | Affects cost or service quality | Directly determines win/loss, licence to operate, or the strategy |
| **Maturity** | Ad hoc, person-dependent, undocumented | Defined and repeatable, some measurement | Measured, optimized, continuously improved |
| **Technology dependence** | Mostly human judgment and relationships | Supported by systems, with manual steps | Cannot function without integrated technology |
| **Performance vs benchmark** | Well below peers or targets | At parity | Clearly better than peers |

## The heat map

Plot **strategic importance** (vertical) against **maturity** (horizontal).

```
 High importance
       │
  INVEST      │   PROTECT
  (gap is     │   (sustain, do not
   strategic; │    over-invest)
   fund it)   │
  ────────────┼──────────────────
  WATCH       │   RATIONALIZE
  (low value; │   (efficient utility;
   question    │    consolidate or
   whether it  │    retire)
   matters)    │
       │
 Low importance        High maturity
```

| Quadrant | Interpretation | Action | Governance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High importance, low maturity** | Strategic gap | Fund a focused initiative; assign a senior owner | Stage-gated investment; quarterly review |
| **High importance, high maturity** | Strategic strength | Protect; maintain; avoid disruption | Service levels; resilience testing |
| **Low importance, low maturity** | Question the need | Stop, outsource, or accept the risk explicitly | Light touch; annual review |
| **Low importance, high maturity** | Over-engineered utility | Standardize, consolidate, reduce cost | Cost targets; rationalization |

## Worked example: Meridian Retail

| Capability | Importance | Maturity | Tech dependence | Performance | Owner | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fulfil from store | 5 | 2 | 5 | Below | COO | Distributed order management (H2) |
| Handle returns | 5 | 1 | 4 | Below | COO | Returns portal and reverse logistics (H2) |
| Take payment | 5 | 4 | 5 | At | CFO | Protect; PCI remediation (H1) |
| Price and promote | 4 | 3 | 4 | At | CMO | Improve promotion engine (H3) |
| Manage store stock | 4 | 2 | 4 | Below | COO | Include in WMS review (H2) |
| Run payroll | 2 | 5 | 3 | At | HR | Rationalize to shared service (H1) |
| Manage facilities | 1 | 4 | 1 | At | Facilities | No investment |

**What the table shows.** Two capabilities determine the online strategy and both are immature. Payroll is mature and unimportant: it should not consume scarce engineering time. Facilities management is not a technology capability at all, and should be removed from the IT portfolio conversation.

## Quality checks

- [ ] Every capability is named as a verb phrase ("price a parcel"), not a system.
- [ ] The map has 8–12 domains and no more than 120 capabilities.
- [ ] Each division's leadership team has reviewed its own domain.
- [ ] Every capability scoring 4–5 on importance has a named business owner.
- [ ] Every high-importance, low-maturity capability has a gap action and a horizon.
- [ ] The bottom-left quadrant has at least one candidate for retirement.
- [ ] Scores are defensible: I can give the evidence for each 1 and each 5.

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Listing applications instead of capabilities | Map becomes an asset register | Rewrite each entry as "we can…" |
| Everything scored 4 or 5 | No prioritization possible | Force-rank; allow no more than 20% at 5 |
| IT-only workshop | Map is accurate and ignored | Co-create with divisional leaders |
| No owners | Gaps stay open | Name a business owner per capability |
| Confusing maturity with importance | Investment goes to mature, unimportant areas | Score the two attributes independently |
| Never refreshed | Map describes a company that no longer exists | Refresh annually with the strategy cycle |
