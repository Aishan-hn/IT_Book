# Strategy on a Page

**Use when:** you need a strategy that a board can read in ninety seconds and a CFO can interrogate in five minutes.
**Time:** 12 weeks to produce honestly; 2 hours to draft a first version that will be wrong.
**Output:** one page containing a diagnosis, an ambition, choices, initiatives, a stop list, costs, measures, and an ask.

---

## Field guide

| Field | What good looks like | What bad looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation** | Three specific, numeric facts that make the current path untenable | "The pace of change is accelerating" |
| **Ambition** | One outcome, dated, measurable | "Become a digital leader" |
| **How we win** | Comparative claim plus a named trade-off | A list of five priorities |
| **Initiatives** | Seven or fewer, each with an owner and a measure | Forty projects, no owners |
| **Stop list** | Real things people are currently doing | Empty, or "reduce duplication" |
| **Cost** | Envelope by year, plus a pre-agreed cut rule | "To be determined in the budget cycle" |
| **Measures** | Baseline, target, date, owner | Activity counts |
| **Ask** | One decision, one date, one decider | "Please support the strategy" |

## Worked example

```
IT STRATEGY ON A PAGE — Meridian Group
Version 1.0   Owner: P. Raman   Approved: ExCo, 14 March   Review: quarterly

1. SITUATION
   • Online is growing 40% a year but costs 30% more to serve than store;
     returns run at 24% of orders.
   • 62% of the €58m run budget supports applications tied to under 15% of revenue.
   • The core banking batch depends on one person and has never been
     recovery-tested end to end.
   • The board wants growth and €40m of group cost reduction in the same three years.

2. AMBITION
   By the end of year three, online is 30% of retail revenue at or below
   store-level contribution margin, and IT run cost is 18% lower in real terms.

3. HOW WE WILL WIN
   1. Fulfilment speed and accuracy in Retail — the constraint on online growth.
   2. Cost-to-serve leadership in Logistics — the only durable margin lever.
   3. Ring-fenced resilience in Financial Services — the licence to operate.
   Trade-off: store technology refresh is deferred for two years.

4. WHAT WE WILL DO
   1. Distributed order management — COO — online contribution margin 4% → 9%, Q4 Y2 — €3.1m — H1/H2
   2. Returns and reverse logistics — COO — returns cost per order −25%, Q2 Y2 — €1.4m — H2
   3. Route sequencing upgrade — Logistics MD — cost per parcel −4 cents, Q3 Y1 — €0.9m — H1
   4. Core banking DR and batch de-risking — CISO/CFO — DR test passed, Q2 Y1 — €2.2m — H1
   5. Application rationalization — IT Director — 22 systems → 14, Q4 Y2 — €1.6m — H1/H2
   6. Cloud landing zone and wave 2 — IT Director — 40% workloads, cost per transaction −15%, Q4 Y2 — €4.0m — H2
   7. Cost transparency and chargeback — CFO — 100% of IT cost allocated monthly, Q1 Y1 — €0.3m — H1

5. WHAT WE WILL STOP
   Store refresh programme; two reporting tools; the innovation lab;
   three divisional shadow SaaS contracts.

6. COST AND FUNDING
   Year 1 €11m capex / €0.8m opex; Year 2 €9m; Year 3 €7m.
   Funded from the group transformation envelope.
   If the envelope falls 15%, we defer initiative 6 by two quarters and cut 5 to 16 systems.

7. HOW WE WILL KNOW
   Online contribution margin   4%      9%      Q4 Y2   COO
   Cost per parcel              €4.20   €4.16   Q3 Y1   Logistics MD
   IT run cost per employee     €12.9k  €10.6k  Q4 Y3   CFO
   DR test pass rate            0%      100%    Q2 Y1   CISO

8. WHAT WE NEED
   Decision: approve the envelope and the stop list.
   By when: 31 March.   From whom: ExCo.
```

## Quality checks

- [ ] A director can read it in ninety seconds and state the strategy back to you.
- [ ] The diagnosis contains numbers, not adjectives.
- [ ] There are no more than seven initiatives.
- [ ] The stop list is non-empty and specific.
- [ ] Every initiative has a named owner who has agreed to it.
- [ ] Every measure has a baseline, a target, and a date.
- [ ] The cost section states the envelope and the pre-agreed cut rule.
- [ ] The ask is a decision, not an endorsement.
- [ ] The page has been challenged by finance and risk before the board sees it.
- [ ] The strategy has a standing quarterly review slot with the power to change it.

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Writing the page last, from a long document | It reads like a summary, not a strategy | Write the page first; the long document is evidence |
| More than seven initiatives | No focus; nothing finishes | Merge, defer, or kill until seven remain |
| No stop list | New work piles onto old work | Name what stops, with an owner and a date |
| Measures without baselines | Improvement cannot be claimed | Measure before you start |
| Aspirational ambition | Nobody knows when it is achieved | Date it and quantify it |
| Asking for support | Nothing happens | Ask for a specific decision from a named person by a named date |
| Never revisited | Strategy diverges from reality | Quarterly review, standing agenda item |
